# 11 — Contested Hypotheses

Three families of claim about the pyramids sit outside mainstream scholarship. They are grouped here because they fail for different reasons, and because the way each fails is more instructive than the claim itself.

## The pyramid as an energy machine

**The claim.** Christopher Dunn's *The Giza Power Plant* (1998), extended in *Giza: The Tesla Connection* (2024), proposes that the Great Pyramid was built as a machine for harvesting and distributing energy — harmonically coupled to the Earth, harvesting electron flow from stressed igneous rock, with the internal chambers forming a coupled-oscillator system [91].

**What is offered.** A specific physical mechanism, an experiment performed by Dunn's model, and a body of internal measurements: acoustic testing at ancient sites, the claimed scorch marks on the Grand Gallery ceiling, the 2017 Big Void, and the possibility that the void housed a counterweight [91].

**The specific problems.**

- *The counterweight.* Dunn's reading of the Grand Gallery as a counterweight shaft requires that a counterweight be able to descend and be recovered. Dunn himself notes the difficulty: the Subterranean Chamber, where such a system would terminate, has no exit anywhere, and when explorers cleared and chiselled out the well shaft in the chamber's centre, they found no exit downward [91]. A system that cannot be reset cannot be a repeating machine.
- *Scale.* The power claimed for a single pyramid is, on Dunn's own account, a small fraction of the power of a mobile phone [91].
- *The plateau problem.* If all the Giza pyramids performed the same function, they would require a central, very high-powered pulse generator serving the entire plateau — a device that would have to be far larger than any pyramid and for which there is no evidence at all [91].
- *Falsifiability.* Dunn's own framing of the method is that "all evidence has to be explained" by the hypothesis, or the hypothesis must be revised [91]. That is a commitment to the theory, not to the evidence — the inverse of a scientific criterion, and the reason the hypothesis has not moved into the peer-reviewed literature despite decades of work and multiple books.

**Grade: E.** Not because the pyramid has no interesting physical properties — the seismic data in [Chapter 6](06-seismic-performance.md) is genuinely remarkable — but because the energy-machine hypothesis makes claims that have been made testable and have not passed. The honest summary is that the pyramid *is* a remarkable acoustic and seismic structure; that is a different and better-supported claim than the one made in its name.

## The pyramid as cast concrete

**The claim.** Joseph Davidovits, a French materials scientist, has argued since the late 1970s that the Great Pyramid's blocks are not carved but **cast** — a re-agglomerated limestone bonded with a geopolymer binder, carried to site as a slurry and packed into reusable wooden moulds [18][92].

**This one deserves more care than the energy-machine hypothesis**, because it was taken seriously by materials scientists rather than only by popular authors, and because the evidence genuinely looked interesting for a while.

**The history.** Davidovits proposed the theory in 1978–79. At the 1984 Symposium on Archaeometry at the Smithsonian, the theory met "polite but stony opposition from archaeological scientists" [93]. His proof-of-concept worked: a team of five to ten using simple hand tools could agglomerate five blocks of 1.3–4.5 t in a couple of weeks [18]. In 2006, Michel Barsoum and colleagues at Drexel University published scanning electron microscopy work in the *Journal of the American Ceramic Society* identifying mineral compounds and air bubbles in pyramid limestone samples that, they argued, do not occur in natural limestone [18][94]. That paper was the strongest single blow for the hypothesis, and it is the paper the opposition has focused on since.

**Why it fails.**

- *The petrographic counter-examination.* Dipayan Jana, in the Proceedings of the Twenty-ninth Conference on Cement Microscopy in 2007, concluded that "we are far from accepting even as a remote possibility a 'man-made' origin of pyramid stones" [18]. The key sample — the "Lauer sample", from the collection of Auguste Mariette — was re-analysed by geologists who concluded that the apparent "geopolymer signature" was absent and that the casing stone is of geological origin [95].
- *The granite problem.* The hypothesis does not explain the granite, weighing well over 10 t, above the King's Chamber — which Davidovits concedes were carved [18]. A theory that explains 90% of a building and not the part that is hardest to make is not a theory of the building.
- *The counter-arguments.* Opponents also note that the diverse shapes of the stones show that moulds were not used, and that a huge amount of limestone chalk and burnt wood would have been needed for the concrete, when the Egyptians had the manpower to hoist natural stone [94].
- *What the geometry actually shows.* Independent petrographic observations of the Great Pyramid's building stones find that the blocks are irregular in shape with edges and corners not preserved, spaces between them not always filled with mortar, and fragments of limestone of different shapes and sizes within the mortar — the signature of quarried and broken stone, not cast mouldings [96]. Further work distinguishes at least three limestone types, with one used for the core and another for the covering [96].

**Grade: E for the general claim, with a real point underneath it.** The hypothesis is not refuted in the sense of being disproven; it is *not supported*, and the specific sample that carried it has been examined and found not to support it [95]. The point underneath it is worth keeping: it is correct that the core is rough, interstitial and mortar-packed rather than precision masonry [9], and that the precision was spent on the casing [10]. The hypothesis takes a real observation and attaches it to a mechanism that the evidence does not support.

Note the structural parallel with the energy-machine claim: both are responses to a genuine unsolved problem (how were the blocks raised and set, and how was the core filled), and both resolve the difficulty by removing the difficulty rather than by explaining it.

## The lost civilisation

**The claim.** A vanished advanced civilisation built the pyramids, and their knowledge was lost.

**Status: E, and the least serious of the three, because it requires discarding the entire documentary and archaeological record.**

The pyramids are directly dated. They are directly inscribed. The workforce's graves are excavated, with their names, their ration records, their medical care and their work-gang marks [68][69][70][12]. The quarries they were cut from are identified and their production techniques are reconstructed from the tools left behind [15][16]. The ramps they were hauled up have been excavated elsewhere at the same date [43]. The site's administration is documented in papyri [20][19].

There is no residue of this hypothesis to discuss, because there is no gap in the record for it to fill. It is not a fringe position within the field; it is a position outside it, and the field's evidence does not merely outweigh it — it makes it unavailable.

## Where the honest uncertainty lives

The three hypotheses above fail in different ways, and the differences are informative.

The **energy machine** fails on physics and on its own internal accounting. It is a claim about a mechanism, and the mechanism does not close.

The **cast concrete** fails on materials science, specifically on the one sample that carried it. It is a claim about a material, and the material is natural.

The **lost civilisation** fails on the record. It is a claim about history, and the history is written down.

None of them addresses the genuine open questions, which are questions about *how* rather than *whether*: the ramp geometry, the raising mechanism, the void functions, the workforce scale. Those are addressed in [Chapter 10](10-evidence-grading-and-open-questions.md), and every one of them has a named measurement that would close it.

That contrast is the point worth taking away. The pyramid's mysteries are not hidden; they are *specific*. They name a location, a date, a quantity and a method. The difference between a genuine open question and a pseudo-mystery is exactly that difference — and the pyramid is unusually rich in the first kind, because the Egyptian state was literate enough to make ordinary administration recordable, and arid enough to preserve what it recorded.

## Grade summary

| Hypothesis | Grade | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Pyramid as an energy-generating machine | E | Mechanism fails its own closure test; counterweight path has no exit; plateau-scale generator required and unevidenced [91] |
| Pyramid core cast from geopolymer concrete | E (general) / C (residual observation) | Key sample re-analysed and found geological; hypothesis does not cover granite [18][95] |
| Lost advanced civilisation | E | Contradicted by the dated, inscribed and excavated record [20][43][68] |
| Core masonry is interstitial and rough, precision spent on casing | C | Documented petrography and survey [9][10][96] |
| The pyramid has genuine seismic and acoustic properties | A | Measured [52] |
