# 10 — Evidence Grading and Open Questions

This chapter does two jobs. It states the rules used to grade every claim elsewhere in the repository, and it names the questions that remain open — precisely enough that each one can be closed by a specific measurement.

## The grading scale

The scale measures evidentiary strength, not interest, novelty or plausibility.

| Grade | Definition | Typical examples in this repository |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Direct measurement, or a peer-reviewed finding derived from primary data and independently replicated | Alignment error [61]; friction coefficient [31]; SP-NFC dimensions [77]; the 2.3 Hz / 0.6 Hz frequency split [52] |
| **B** | Peer-reviewed model or interpretation, not yet confirmed by independent observation | Houdin's internal ramp [47]; the ratchet load path [6]; the sliding-ramp re-reading [51] |
| **C** | Scholarly inference from primary archaeological data, with the reasoning stated and checkable | Plug stones lowered from within the pyramid [57]; relieving chambers diverting load [6]; the workforce range [40][41][42] |
| **D** | Falsifiable hypothesis, live in the literature, currently unsupported by direct evidence | Function of the Big Void; the Trial Passages as an observatory [63]; Hawass's 30 m corridor [86] |
| **E** | Speculative or pseudo-scientific claim: contradicted by the evidence, or not testable as stated | The pyramid as an energy machine [91]; the pyramid as cast concrete [18] |

Two disciplines make this scale work. First, a claim's grade can differ by dimension: the Big Void's **existence** is A while its **function** is D. Second, a claim is never graded on its rhetorical quality. A hypothesis that is beautifully argued and has no supporting evidence is still D.

## Why the grading is not a disclaimer

Every serious field that has been invaded by confident amateurs has the same failure mode: a public claim migrates from "no one has checked this" to "it is true" through sheer repetition, because nobody records what has and has not been tested.

The pyramid field is a textbook case. The 2017 Big Void announcement produced a flurry of "the pyramid hides a secret chamber" claims that the primary literature never made [76][77]. The 2025–2026 cycle repeated it with a "30 m corridor ending in a sealed door" [86][87], and produced a 2022 satellite-radar paper that was retracted for serious methodological flaws and statistical errors [89][90], alongside Khafre "underground city" claims that were never submitted for peer review at all [89].

The grading system is a structural defence against that. If a reader can see, at a glance, that "SP-NFC dimensions" is A and "Big Void function" is D and "energy machine" is E, then the migration cannot happen silently.

## The named open questions

### 1. The geometry of the upper ramps

**Status:** genuinely open. No scholarly consensus exists on the ramp system used [1][44].

**What we know:** a straight ramp to the summit would require roughly 8 million m³ of fill — over three times the pyramid's volume — and about 1.4 km of length, with no room on the plateau and no surviving remains [30][14]. A wrapping spiral solves the material problem but hides the corners the builders needed to sight [44][46]. An internal spiral solves both but assumes undocumented corridors and is contested by Egyptologists [47]. The Hatnub quarry ramp demonstrates the technique at Khufu's date, with a measured geometry of 7.8 m width, ~80 m length and up to 20% gradient [43][10].

**What would close it:** microgravimetric or full-density mapping across the pyramid volume at sufficient resolution to detect a ramp void [45]; direct excavation of the space between the Grand Gallery and the core; or recovery of ramp debris at a scale matching the models. The 2026 computational framework frames the zero-footprint edge-integrated ramp as a "falsifiable prediction, not evidence" and identifies where to look [46].

**Common error:** treating the absence of ramp debris as proof that no ramp existed. Copas's formulation is the correct one — the absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and the usual recycling explanation "has itself not been independently verified" [1].

### 2. The mechanism for raising blocks in the upper third

**Status:** open, and tightly coupled to question 1.

**What we know:** the bottom-up construction assumed for the lower courses is a well-understood, well-evidenced procedure [10][43]. Above roughly 43 m, the options narrow dramatically, because the required gradient rises as the courses shrink [1].

**What would close it:** the same three as above. A demonstration that a specific mechanism can place a block at 120 m at a rate consistent with the construction schedule would effectively settle the question by elimination.

### 3. The function of the Big Void and the North Face Corridor

**Status:** existence established at grade A; function at grade D [73][77].

**What we know:** the Big Void has a Grand Gallery-like cross-section, sits above the Grand Gallery, and is at least 30 m long [73][75]. The SP-NFC is a horizontal corridor ~9 m long behind the north-face chevron, with a gabled ceiling and rough walls [77][79]. Mostafa Waziri, then head of the Supreme Council of Antiquities, said the corridor was most likely a weight-relieving structure, with the chevron ceiling engineered to deflect the crushing vertical pressure of the upper tiers away from the space below [80].

**What would close it:** physical access. The 2026 robotics effort is aimed at exactly this [84]. A camera at the end of the Big Void would end the speculation in a single afternoon.

**Common error:** treating the most attractive hypothesis as the established one. Load relief is a plausible reading of the geometry, but it has never been tested against alternatives. The competing reading — that the void is a construction artefact, a counterweight shaft or a ramp — has never been tested either.

### 4. The scale of the workforce

**Status:** open in detail, bounded in order of magnitude.

**What we know:** the range across serious estimates is roughly 800-900 (energy floor) to 36,000 (Hawass), with the most commonly cited band being 20,000–30,000 total for the pyramid project [39][40][41][42][40]. The archaeological constraints — settlement size, cemetery scale, food consumption, herder requirements — and the physical constraint — the work-energy bound — are both firm; what they disagree about is what to include.

**What would close it:** a settlement area model that can be tested against excavation data, combined with a seasonal labour levy model calibrated against the documented phyle system and the flood-season logistics [19][41]. In practice this question closes slowly, as more of the settlement is excavated.

**Common error:** the framing itself. The "100,000 slaves versus 20,000 workers" argument is a category error — it compares a second-hand ancient hearsay figure against a modern estimate of a different quantity, and it has been settled in the popular literature for over a decade [66][67].

### 5. The role of the Trial Passages

**Status:** open. Two short passages cut into bedrock southeast of the pyramid, misaligned by about a quarter of a degree east of true north [63].

**What we know:** Lightbody's hypothesis is that they were built for prolonged observation of the circumpolar stars, establishing a north–south reference line for the plateau with roughly four times the accuracy of a single-shot alignment [63]. The hypothesis is explicitly labelled hypothetical by its author, and the claim that the passages are "suspicious" was made about the *idea* of explaining only the pyramid's construction [47].

**What would close it:** excavation of the passages' observing features, and astronomical analysis of the passage geometry against Old Kingdom pole positions.

### 6. The chronology question

**Status:** marginal, and important to state correctly.

Carbon dating of core blocks produced results that averaged 374 years earlier than the accepted date in a 1984 study, and 100–200 years earlier in a 1995 study [44]. These results have been read by a minority as evidence for a much older pyramid.

The mainstream position is that the pyramid dates to Khufu's reign — he reigned from 2509 to 2483 BCE [74], and the construction is generally placed around 2600 BCE over about 26 years [6]. The W-collapse hypothesis and the associated geological arguments are not accepted, and the dating discrepancy is generally attributed to the difficulty of dating limestone with residual carbon, old limestone from the Mokattam formation, and contamination effects.

**Why it is in this repository:** because a reader who meets the carbon-dating results will meet claims built on them. The correct response is not dismissal but proportion: the dating results are interesting and anomalous; the extraordinary conclusions drawn from them are not supported.

### 7. The purpose of the Subterranean Chamber

**Status:** open. Cut into the bedrock and left unfinished [6][52]. The usual suggestions are that the excavation was halted when the builders realised the bedrock did not permit completion, or that the chamber's dimensions were determined by the availability of suitable rock rather than by design intent. No textual evidence resolves it.

## The three questions that would change the most

If only three things were measured, these are the ones with the highest information gain:

1. **A camera inside the Big Void.** It would convert the most-discussed unknown in the field into a documented feature, and it would immediately bear on the internal ramp question.
2. **A full density survey of the pyramid volume.** It is the only measurement that can discriminate between external ramp, internal ramp and no-ramp models without excavation, because the models predict different density signatures at different locations.
3. **Provenance sampling of core, backing and casing stone.** It would settle whether the core was quarried, and it is the measurement that the "pyramid concrete" hypothesis of [Chapter 11](11-contested-hypotheses.md) has been unable to survive.

## A note on the argument's own limits

This repository is not a neutral instrument. It argues that the pyramid is an industrial and engineering achievement whose open questions are narrow and nameable. Two ways that argument could be wrong:

- **If the upper courses were placed by a mechanism nobody has proposed.** The energy bound shows the work required is not extraordinary, but the work could still have been done in a way that leaves no trace and no model. The absence of large ramp debris at Giza is consistent with that.
- **If the datable record is systematically misleading.** If the carbon-dating results reflect something other than contamination, the entire chronology of the pyramid's programme would need revisiting. The mainstream reading of the dating results makes this unlikely, but it is the one place where the argument depends on a single contested interpretation rather than on converging evidence.

Both are stated here rather than left implicit, because a repository that grades everyone else's evidence and not its own is not doing the thing it claims to do.
